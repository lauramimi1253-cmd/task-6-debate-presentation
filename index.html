The Netherlands:
Tourism & Livability

Should the Dutch government limit tourists and foreign property investors in major cities to protect residents' quality of life?

Position: Targeted national limits — not blanket bans.
National Context

Not just Amsterdam — the whole country

The pressure from tourism and investors affects Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague, Utrecht, and student cities like Eindhoven and Groningen.

Arguments FOR

Why the Netherlands should act

🏠

Housing

Returning buy-to-let homes to Dutch families reduces the shortage.

🌿

Quality of Life

Less noise and overcrowding in historic centers.

🏘️

Social Fabric

Teachers and nurses should afford to live in their cities.

Arguments AGAINST

Why we must be careful

💶

Economy

Tourism contributes around 4% of Dutch GDP and creates jobs.

📈

Investment

The Netherlands relies on foreign investment and talent.

⚖️

Enforcement

Rules must be applied by 342 municipalities under EU law.

National Solutions

Targeted, national, and local

  • 1
    Buy-to-let ban: A national law (2022) lets every municipality ban investor purchases in designated areas.
  • 2
    Higher transfer tax: Raised to 10.4% for investors (vs. 2% for owner-occupiers).
  • 3
    Affordable Rent Act: Regulates mid-market rents nationally (July 2024).
  • 4
    National housing target: 100,000 new homes per year.
Interesting fact: The Netherlands was one of the first EU countries to create a national legal framework for buy-to-let restrictions.
Conclusion

Balance,
not bans.

The Netherlands should apply targeted, national and locally enforced limits — not blanket prohibitions — to protect housing and livability.
